01About me

From cartilage mechanics
to experimental systems.

Osteoarthritis involves structural and functional deterioration of joint tissues, including degeneration of articular cartilage. My research examines how the synovial fluid environment influences cartilage’s resistance to fatigue failure under repeated loading.

My dissertation investigates the mechanical interaction between synovial fluid and articular cartilage. I use controlled loading and bath-composition experiments to separate fatigue protection from frictional sliding, identify contributing fluid constituents, and investigate how that protection acts.

A substantial part of this work is engineering the experiments themselves. I designed and built solenoid-driven compression and voice-coil tensile fatigue systems, integrating specimen fixtures, force and displacement sensors, and tensile force feedback. Developing, troubleshooting, and validating these systems enables controlled tests tailored to questions that standard commercial equipment does not adequately address.

Understanding how native cartilage functions and fails also informs my contributions to NITRO / NOVAJoint. Within this collaborative effort toward biological knee replacement, I work on initial scaffold design and fabrication, and evaluate frictional and compressive properties against native joint tissue.

02Research & engineering

Research highlights

PhD research · Experimental mechanics of cartilage

How does synovial fluid protect cartilage from fatigue?

Repeated joint loading can produce fatigue damage in articular cartilage. My research investigates how the surrounding fluid modifies this failure process, with relevance to the mechanical deterioration of cartilage in osteoarthritis.

Across four major research areas, I connect custom instrument development with controlled experiments: establishing fatigue protection, identifying contributing constituents, probing mechanism, and testing a different loading mode. Paired specimens and controlled bath conditions help isolate these effects in immature bovine cartilage.

Research areas

Four connected investigations explored during my PhD.

01 / RESEARCH

Published study

Fatigue protection without sliding.

I developed a solenoid-driven compression tester to isolate cartilage fatigue from frictional sliding and test how synovial fluid concentration affects protection.

Approach & contribution
  • Experimental design: paired immature bovine cartilage specimens tested in synovial fluid (SF), phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), and SF/PBS dilutions. Pairing and anatomical allocation reduced specimen variability.
  • Loading and measurement: approximately 54 N at 2 Hz for up to 36,000 cycles, with continuous force recording. Damage was evaluated using surface profilometry, contact-area measurements, polarized light microscopy, and histology.
  • Published result: all eight PBS specimens failed by 12,000 cycles, while their eight paired SF specimens remained intact through 36,000 cycles under these conditions.
Read the publication
02 / RESEARCH

Manuscript under review

Identifying protective fluid constituents.

I use constituent-addition and metal-cation-chelation experiments to investigate the contributions of Mg²⁺ and Ca²⁺ to fatigue protection in devitalized immature bovine cartilage.

Approach & contribution
  • Approach: compare defined bath solutions containing candidate proteins, phospholipids, or divalent cations with SF, and test how EDTA chelation changes protection.
  • Experimental controls: paired specimens, bath-composition controls, and attention to pH and osmolarity, including osmolarity-matched SF controls for chelation experiments.
  • Status: the completed study identifies Mg²⁺ and Ca²⁺ as major mediators under the tested conditions. The first-author manuscript is under review at Osteoarthritis and Cartilage.

03 / RESEARCH

Mechanistic investigation

Mechanisms and reversibility of protection.

I investigate whether SF changes the thermal response of cartilage and whether fatigue protection persists after SF is removed.

Approach & contribution
  • Thermal response: controlled heat treatment followed by unconfined compression measures changes in equilibrium modulus as an indirect marker of collagen denaturation.
  • Reversibility: paired specimens receive the same SF preincubation, then undergo cyclic compression in either PBS or SF.
  • Status: thermal-stability testing remains ongoing; preliminary data did not detect an SF-dependent shift at the temperatures tested. Bath-switching experiments support a requirement for continued SF exposure during loading. The manuscript is in preparation.

04 / RESEARCH

Ongoing research

Testing protection in tensile fatigue.

I built a voice-coil-actuated tensile fatigue tester to determine whether SF and its divalent cations protect cartilage when loaded directly in tension.

Approach & contribution
  • System: voice-coil actuation, load-cell-based force feedback, LVDT displacement measurement, custom specimen clamps, and an immersion bath for controlled cyclic tensile loading.
  • Study design: dog-bone-shaped cartilage specimens with superficial-zone collagen fibers aligned with loading, and bath assignments balanced across anatomical harvest locations.
  • Outcome: cycles to rupture within the gauge region. The platform has been developed; whether SF and the cations extend tensile fatigue life remains under investigation.

DESIGNED & BUILT IN THE LAB

Custom fatigue testing systems

I designed and built two experimental platforms to isolate loading modes, control the fluid environment, and measure cartilage response beyond the capabilities of a standard test setup.

01 / COMPRESSION

Compression fatigue testing

Solenoid actuation isolates repeated compression from frictional sliding. Force recording and LVDT displacement sensing track loading and specimen response.

02 / TENSION

Tensile fatigue testing

Voice-coil actuation, custom grips, and a fluid bath enable cyclic tensile loading. Load-cell feedback regulates force; an LVDT records displacement.

Live closed-loop force control

Force feedback during cyclic loading

The load cell measures force. The PID controller uses the target-minus-measured error, its history, and its rate of change to update the Arduino PWM command to the voice-coil drive.

PWM commands duty cycle, not force. For an 8-bit command, 0 corresponds to 0% and 255 to 100% duty cycle. Resulting force depends on the supply, drive, actuator, and mechanical system.

Independent displacement sensing. The LVDT records displacement; load-cell force closes the control loop.

NITRO / NOVAJoint · Ongoing research

Scaffold mechanics for a living knee replacement

I contribute to Task 1: initial scaffold design and fabrication for 3D-printed biological knee implants. We evaluate friction coefficients and compressive properties against native joint tissue to guide the development of a mechanically functional scaffold.

This collaborative project extends my work on native cartilage mechanics to scaffold design and performance evaluation. NOVAJoint is part of ARPA-H’s NITRO program—Novel Innovations for Tissue Regeneration in Osteoarthritis—and aims to create a living knee replacement that can regenerate cartilage and bone.

Engineering goal A scaffold that supports joint loading and articulation while enabling a lasting biological replacement.
